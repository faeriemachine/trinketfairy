About Me

I’m Eddie, I’m a gay androgynous man and I am disabled with multiple physical, developmental, and psychological conditions. I live in so-called Canada on stolen Algonquin-Anishinaabe land.

I’m autistic, and plushies are easily my biggest special interest. I’ve been collecting them on and off since my early childhood, and have interests in multiple different toy lines. I tend to jump from one brand to another, so I don’t have any set brand that I’m entirely dedicated to. I also collect for my own enjoyment only, and don’t care about the potential profit that I could get from my collection. My special interest also extends to the toy industry itself, including concept designing, production and manufacturing, and marketing tactics.

I use toys and my collection to cope with living in an ableist world as well. I like to think that my collection represents the importance of disabled people having the ability to indulge in any hobby they desire without having to worry about their livelihoods being at risk. Many disabled people are forced into poverty due to the asset limits we get from being on benefits, leaving little room for indulging in our interests and hobbies, despite the fact that having interests is generally beneficial for everyone. If abled people can spend hundreds to thousands on vacations, luxury clothes and social events, then disabled people also deserve to have nice things for the sole reason of “just because.”

I also like to see my collection as being symbolic of the idea that adults don’t have to grow out of the childish traits that make them happy if they don’t want to, and that being childish and having child-like interests isn’t inherently a shameful or negative thing. Having childish traits or interests as an adult isn't a sign of incompetence, believe it or not, and just because I’m an adult, doesn’t mean I have to drop all of my most important interests for the sake of conformity. Personally, I'm of the belief that it's much more immature to conform to others' close-minded opinions than it is to just be yourself. Plus, I think us disabled people are held to unfair expectations to not ‘self-infantilize,’ which extends to abled people believing they have the right to control what interests we partake in, as to not appear ‘too childish.’ While this may come from good intentions, I believe that this in itself is a form of infantilization through assumptions that we cannot make our own decisions on the hobbies we partake in as disabled adults. It’s also incredibly hypocritical, when abled people are allowed to like playing video games, going to Disney, and watching cartoons without being accused of infantilizing themselves.

Outside of plushies, I like a lot of other things too. I'm currently working towards my goal of becoming a freelance artist and fursuit maker, but I also like to keep myself busy with other activities, such as cooking and baking, coding, video editing, thrifting, journaling, kandi-making, photography, hiking, tarot, and watching shows and movies. Other interests of mine include amusement parks, planes and trains, Linux (and tech in general,) horror and unfiction media, virtual reality, raves, metaphysics, and animals. Some day, I'd love to get into JS and Python programming, activism and volunteering, and making long-form videos.

Listening to music is another big passion of mine, and I love all different kinds of genres. I find myself biased towards rock, metal, industrial, electronic, and hip hop music the most, but I'll listen to just about anything.

Favourite toy brands: Sunlemon, Douglas Cuddle Toys, Save Our Space/Leosco, Aurora World, Webkinz (incl. Signatures & Junior,) Prime Shiba, Beanie Babies/Buddies, Furby, Mary Meyer, (vintage) Build A Bear, Charlie Bears...
