Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get into collecting?

Having a goal in mind is your first step, in my opinion. Ask yourself why you want to collect and what exactly you'd like for your collection to look like. Do you want to collect realistic stuffed animals? Plushies of your favourite characters? Do you want to collect from one specific brand or from multiple? Are you a completionist or do you just want to collect what you like? It can also help to make a list of items you'd like to have in your collection and keeping track of your purchases with spreadsheets to keep yourself focused and in check with your spendings.

When it comes to getting items on your ISO list, it can help to be persistent but patient, you will find your grails eventually if you offer yourself enough time and restraint! Start by widening your scope of where you get collection pieces—search online for potential nearby retailers of the brand(s) you wish to collect if they're still in production, or for other businesses across your country if you're willing to get them shipped.

I also recommend making a habit of checking your local thrift stores frequently enough to get an idea of what kind of items they tend to get, if you're okay with getting items secondhand. Similarly, turn on email alerts on sites like Ebay to keep track of when the items you're searching for get listed and for how much. It's also a good practice to view sold listings for items you want to see how much they sell for—just because an item may be listed for a lot, doesn't mean it's actually getting sold for that price!

Starting a new collection can be equal parts exciting and daunting, especially when you're starting from nothing and have so many items you want that it starts becoming overwhelming. That is okay, but it's important to curb one's expectations. You won't be able to get everything you want, and you don't have to start by getting as many items as you can. As much as it might not seem like it from how bad reselling has become, you are in control of how much you spend, and it's important to practice learning restraint when it comes to the lengths you're willing to go to obtain a grail.

How did YOU start collecting? Why do you collect?

I've been collecting plushies and toys for as far back as I can remember, with it being one of the first special interests I'd develop as young as 4 years old when I received my childhood plush, Pilchard. It was easily my favourite way to engage with my interests, with me collecting toys from my favourite media and plushies of my favourite animals. I adored and cherished every single plush I had back then, and I could easily say that they were my whole world.

Over time, I'd unfortunately get stuck on the idea that having childish interests was a sign of emotional immaturity, and began to distance myself from it when I was around 12 years old. I'd come back around to it eventually, where I'd start rebuilding my Webkinz collection around 2015 (90% of which is entirely thrifted plush), and I'd realize all over again how important plushies and toys are to my life. Still, these fears of being seen as immature affected me to the point where I gave away 95% of my childhood collection, something that I still grieve to this day.

As for why I collect, there's a number of reasons. For one, I feel like plushies are portable sources of comfort that can come with you anywhere and are symbols of retaining child-like wonder and joy into our every day adult lives. Our world is shitty and stressful, with many of us being driven to burnout because we don't allow ourselves to de-stress and process our emotions—both of which are things that are skills learned through play when we're young. I also personally find it crazy that in every other species, adults consistently continue to play, yet we're expected to grow out of it by the time we hit a certain age. Conformity is not only boring, but also confusing to me, and even when I was under other peoples' influence, I never completely understood why I was obligated to follow these arbitrary rules.

I also find that plushies are able to hold my attention for much longer than anything else in this world, and I often spend hours researching about them and spending time with my plushies. The great thing about an interest like this is that you will never run out of new things to learn about, which I think has helped keep me interested in them for as long as I have.

Outside of that, I don't believe my love for plushies needs to have a deeper meaning. I like plushies just because I think they're cute and soothing, and it reasons don't need to be more elaborate than that.

Where do you get your toys from?

I'm from Canada, so most of my toys are bought locally or shipped within the country from sites like Ebay and Poshmark due to how abysmal our shipping rates are. Rarely I'll buy from places like Kijiji (Canadian Craigslist equivalent) and Facebook Marketplace on the off chance I find something from there.

I also spend a lot of time searching for and scouting out new locations to buy toys from, and sticking to those locations for most of my purchases. Also thrift stores— they're very helpful when it comes to retired toys and I visit them frequently, but I think the fact that I live in a city influences my thrift store finds.

Where do you get your gear from?

Some of it I make myself, and some of it I get from other sources like Etsy, local pet stores and thrift stores. Some of my stuff was from Amazon, but I'm trying not to buy from them if I can help it.

Please remember that it takes a lot of time to build up a good amount of gear! I also highly recommend making your own whenever possible.

How many plushies/toys do you own?

I have no idea! I only have 150+ of them registered on my spreadsheet, but this only includes plushies, and ones that aren't based on existing characters.

What camera do you use for your photography?

I use a Canon Rebel T3 DSLR and a Canon Powershot ELPH 180 digicam the most. On occasion, I'll also use the camera on my Samsung Galaxy S20 FE.

What is Mended Wings?

“Mended Wings” is what I call the role-playing headworld that I have with my plushies, which centers around the establishment named Mended Wings: Home for Toys in Need. As a method of play, I like to imagine that a lot of my plushies are disabled in some way, and live in a commune where they help each other out with each others’ needs. In universe, Mended Wings operates much like co-op housing, where each member is expected to contribute to the local community in exchange for subsidized housing and free services.

Within the community of Mended Wings are a group of plushies who have more specific jobs that are centered around how they support me. Their working title is the Emotional Support Crew (or ESC for short,) but I’m trying to think of a cooler name for them. Regardless, these plushies are given special roles based on what parts of my recovery they represent to me. Some hold certain memories, some help with emotional pain, some help with physical pain, and some may even provide me with spiritual healing. Whatever their job may be, they’re the most important plush in my life.

Why do you give your plushies ages, genders/sexualities, and disabilities?

I see my plushies being akin to real people, who are individual, and in specific cases, sentient or conscious. Thus, they have their own identities as any person would, including their own age, gender expression, orientation, and ability.

It's also generally something I think is very fun to do with my plushies. I'm someone who loves to create, so I use my plushies as faceclaims for what are essentially just original characters at this point that happen to look like my plushies.

Why do you dress your plushies in service animal gear?

I am legally disabled on a federal and provincial level and would be legible for a service dog on multiple fronts, including but not limited to; Autism, chronic pain & fatigue, C-PTSD, psychosis, and dissociation. However, due to slashes to our healthcare system by our provincial government, the waitlist for a service dog would be long and difficult.

Service dog ownership is animal ownership, and outside of owning a cat for emotional support, this requires time, energy and space that I currently do not have. I'm hoping to one day seek out my options for a service dog, but I need to make sure I'm going to be responsible with my ownership and that a service dog is the right fit for me. Until then, I use plushies to help me with a variety of different tasks.

For clarity sake—no, I don't believe that my stuffed animals can task themselves. I don't believe that my stuffed animals are anywhere on the same level as a real service dog. I just do this because I'm a disabled person who believes that it's a fun, cute, and harmless thing to do.
